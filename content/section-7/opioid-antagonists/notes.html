Chapter 34 · Opioid antagonists

Pure opioid antagonists

Drugs with no agonist action of their own: they block opioid receptors and reverse opioid poisoning.

Naloxone, Naltrexone, Nalmefene

Naloxone

N-allyl-noroxymorphone. A competitive antagonist at all opioid receptors, blocking μ at much lower doses than κ or δ. No agonist action even at 20× the μ-blocking dose.
No opioid on boardNaloxone↓Nothing happensno subjective effects, no dependence

Dose ladder

0.4–0.8 mg IVreverses morphine
0.4 mg IVprecipitates withdrawal in dependence, lasting 2–3 h
4–10 mgreverses nalorphine and pentazocine
No dose worksbuprenorphine

The dysphoric and psychotomimetic effects of pentazocine-like drugs are only partly reversed, because they are σ mediated.

Endogenous opioids
Blocked too: people with high pain tolerance become more pain-sensitive. Partly reverses respiratory depression from N₂O and diazepam.
Kinetics
Inactive orally (first pass) · IV acts in 2–3 min · glucuronidated · t½ 1 h in adults, 3 h in newborns · lasts 1–3 h
Adverse effects
Uncommon: rise in BP, pulmonary oedema

Uses of naloxone

Naloxone
Emergencyreverse the opioid
  • Morphine poisoning — drug of choice0.4–0.8 mg IV every 2–3 min, max 10 mg
  • Neonatal asphyxiaopioids given in labour — 10 µg/kg into the umbilical cord
  • Other opioid overdoseincluding agonist-antagonists, except buprenorphine
Othersmaller doses
  • Postoperative respiratory depression0.1–0.2 mg IV, usually keeping the analgesia
  • Adjunct to intraspinal opioids
  • Diagnosis of opioid dependenceprecipitates withdrawal
  • Partly reverses alcohol intoxication
  • Tried in shock, stroke, spinal injuryvalue uncertain
For your clinicIf an opioid like fentanyl is part of IV sedation, keep naloxone ready next to flumazenil. Naloxone reverses the opioid; flumazenil reverses the benzodiazepine. Watch the patient afterwards: naloxone wears off in 1–3 h.

Other antagonists

Enter the brainNaloxone·Naltrexone·Nalmefeneblock central and peripheral μ actions
Stay outside the brainMethylnaltrexone·Alvimopanblock gut μ actions only, so central analgesia is kept

Naltrexone

Oral, lasts 1–2 days

More potent than naloxone. Opioid blockade therapy for post-addicts, 50 mg/day: a usual opioid shot gives no high and craving fades.

Also cuts alcohol craving — approved to prevent relapse of heavy drinking. Nausea, headache; hepatotoxic at high doses.

Nalmefene

Longest acting

No hepatotoxicity, better oral bioavailability, longer acting than naltrexone.

Methylnaltrexone

Peripheral only

Doesn't cross the blood-brain barrier. Reverses constipation in cancer patients on chronic opioids and in methadone maintenance.

Alvimopan

Peripheral only

Poorly absorbed from the gut and doesn't enter the brain. For postoperative ileus after bowel resection.
