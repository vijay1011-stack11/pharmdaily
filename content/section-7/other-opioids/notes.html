Ceiling = analgesic efficacy, + low to ++++ full. The dose given is the equianalgesic dose.

1. Codeine

Methyl-morphine: a natural opium alkaloid and a partial μ agonist with a low ceiling. It has very little affinity for opioid receptors itself.
Normal metaboliserCodeine↓CYP2D6 demethylation↓Morphine↓Analgesia
CYP2D6 poor metaboliserCodeine↓Little conversion↓No morphine↓Analgesia fails
Analgesia
1/10 of morphine · 60 mg codeine ≈ 600 mg aspirin · mild to moderate pain only
Cough
1/3 of morphine, more selective · subanalgesic 10–30 mg
Kinetics
Oral : parenteral 1 : 2 · acts 4–6 h · no parenteral preparation
Side effects
Constipation prominent, so it is used for diarrhoea. Others milder than morphine; abuse liability low.

Pholcodine, ethylmorphine

Codeine-like

Used mainly as antitussives; said to be less constipating.

Heroin

3× morphine

More lipid-soluble → reaches the brain faster → more euphoric and highly addicting. No therapeutic advantage; banned in most countries.

2. Pethidine (meperidine)

Made in 1939 as an atropine substitute. Chemically unlike morphine, but a μ agonist blocked by naloxone.

Morphine10 mg↓Acts 4–6 h↓Suppresses cough↓Bradycardiamarked miosis and constipation
Pethidine~100 mg↓Acts 2–4 h↓No cough relief↓Tachycardiaantimuscarinic: dry mouth, blurred vision; less miosis, constipation, retention

Why overdose excites instead of depressing

Pethidine
↓liver
Meperidinic acid + a little norpethidine
↓builds up in renal failure, or with MAO inhibitors
Tremors, mydriasis, hyperreflexia, delirium, myoclonus, convulsions
Interactions
Nonselective MAO inhibitors: norpethidine builds up, excitement · SSRIs: serotonin syndrome
Use now
Declined. Controls shivering after anaesthesia or during IV infusions. Dose 50–100 mg IM or SC.

3. Fentanyl and remifentanil

Fentanyl

80–100× morphine

Pethidine congener. Few cardiovascular effects, less histamine release.

Very lipid-soluble: peak 5 min after IV, gone in 30–40 min by redistribution; elimination t½ ~4 h.
Injection for anaesthesia · transdermal patch for cancer and chronic pain, working after ~12 h, changed every 3 days · buccal possible, not oral.

Remifentanil

Fastest

Faster-acting fentanyl congener, lasting only 10–15 min after IV. Used only in anaesthesia.

4. Methadone

Synthetic, chemically unlike morphine but pharmacologically very similar. Its two key features: high oral activity and firm tissue binding.
Single dose acts 4–6 h
↓accumulates in tissues, released slowly
Repeated doses act longer — t½ 24–48 h on chronic use
↓
Hardly any "kick" · slow tolerance · withdrawal starts in 1–2 days, prolonged but milder
Substitution therapy
1 mg methadone replaces 4 mg morphine, 2 mg heroin or 20 mg pethidine, then is withdrawn gradually
Methadone maintenance
40–80 mg/day long-term, so injected morphine or heroin gives no pleasure
Kinetics
Oral : parenteral 1 : 2 · 90% protein bound · CYP3A4 and CYP2B6
Interactions
Rifampicin and phenytoin induce its metabolism → withdrawal in methadone-dependent patients
Other uses
Analgesic 2.5–10 mg oral or IM; occasionally antitussive

Dextropropoxyphene, a weak opioid related to methadone, was banned in India in 2013 after overdoses caused delirium, seizures and cardiotoxicity.

5. Tramadol

Centrally acting atypical opioid. Naloxone only partly reverses it: part of its action is non-opioid (monoamine reuptake block).
Action 1Weak μ agonist↓Opioid analgesianaloxone-reversible part
Action 2↓ Reuptake of NA and 5-HT, ↑ 5-HT release↓Spinal monoaminergic pain inhibitionnaloxone-resistant part

Advantages

  • Insignificant respiratory depression, sedation, constipation, urinary retention
  • Good oral bioavailability; t½ 5–6 h
  • Minimal haemodynamic effects
  • Little dose escalation; low abuse potential

Watch for

  • Nausea and dizziness prominent
  • Lowers seizure threshold: contraindicated in epilepsy
  • With SSRIs: serotonin syndrome
  • Not effective in severe pain
Potency
100 mg IV tramadol ≈ 10 mg IM morphine
Uses
Mild to moderate short-lasting pain: diagnostic procedures, injury, surgery. Also chronic and cancer pain. 50–100 mg oral, IM or slow IV, 4–6 hourly; also with paracetamol.

6. Tapentadol

TramadolWeak μ agonist+NA and 5-HT reuptake blockedmore nausea and vomiting
TapentadolWeak μ binding+NA reuptake blocked > 5-HTmainly noradrenergic; less nausea
Kinetics
Well absorbed orally · glucuronide conjugated · excreted in urine
Use
Moderately severe acute and chronic pain; at least as effective as tramadol. 50–100 mg, 2–4 times a day.
Still risks
Serotonin syndrome with SSRIs · seizures in predisposed patients
For your clinicBefore prescribing tramadol or tapentadol after an extraction, ask about antidepressants (SSRIs) and epilepsy. Both are reasons to pick another analgesic.
