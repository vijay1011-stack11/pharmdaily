Chapter 34 · Morphine

Morphine, the prototype

The main alkaloid in opium and still widely used. A full μ agonist: know its actions and the rest of the chapter is comparisons.

1. CNS actions

Depressant actions: what morphine switches down

Morphine → μ receptors across the CNS
↓each centre it depresses gives one effect
(a) Pain pathway → analgesia
Dull, poorly localised visceral pain relieved better than sharp somatic pain; neuritic pain responds less. Relieves the perception of pain and its emotional part, so the patient tolerates it better. Other sensations stay intact.
↓
(b) Cortex, reticular formation → sedation
Drowsiness, mental dullness, indifference, but no ataxia (unlike hypnotics). No anticonvulsant action: fits may be precipitated.
↓
(c⁠) Limbic, reward pathway → euphoria
Calming, detachment, heavy warm limbs. Unpleasant in normal people without pain; a "high" in pain, anxiety or addicts.
↓
(d) Respiratory centre → respiratory depression
Rate and tidal volume both fall. Marked with other sedatives, or in asthma, COPD, cor pulmonale, liver and kidney disease. Death in poisoning is from respiratory failure.
↓
(e) Cough centre → cough suppression
More sensitive to morphine than the respiratory centre
↓
(f) Hypothalamic thermostat → hypothermia
In cold surroundings
↓
(g) Vasomotor centre → fall in BP
Depressed at higher doses

How the analgesia works

SpinalSubstantia gelatinosa of dorsal horn↓↓ glutamate, substance P↓Pain "gate" closesintrathecal morphine: segmental analgesia
SupraspinalMedulla, periaqueductal grey, limbic, cortex↓Alters pain processing↓Boosts descending inhibition5-HT and NA pathways to the cord

The two sites multiply each other. Morphine also acts on peripheral afferent terminals in injured tissue.

Why it feels good, and why it's addictive

μ agonist inhibits GABAergic neurones in the nucleus accumbens
↓
↑ Dopamine release
↓
Euphoria and reinforcement

κ agonists do the opposite: ↓ dopamine, aversion. Less NA release in the locus coeruleus relieves fear and apprehension.

Stimulant actions: what it switches up

CTZ → nausea and vomiting
Worse if the stomach is full or the patient walks about. Larger doses depress the vomiting centre, so emetics don't work in morphine poisoning.
↓
Edinger-Westphal nucleus → miosis
Removes GABAergic inhibition of the III nerve nucleus. No tolerance: addicts keep constricted pupils.
↓
Vagal centre → bradycardia
↓
Cortex and hippocampus → truncal rigidity, convulsions
Rigidity at high IV doses can hamper breathing.

3. Peripheral actions

(a) CVS

Peripheral resistance — vasodilatation↓
BP on standing — postural hypotension, fainting↓
Heart rate — vagal↓

Vasodilatation comes from histamine release, vasomotor depression and a direct action on vessels; blood shifts from the pulmonary to the systemic circuit. Rate may rise reflexly if BP drops.

(b) GIT: constipation

↓

↓

↓

(c⁠) Other smooth muscle

Biliary

Sphincter of Oddi spasm

Intrabiliary pressure ↑ several fold → biliary colic. Countered by naloxone and nitrates.

Urinary

Detrusor + sphincter tone ↑

Urgency and difficulty passing urine. Ureter contractions ↑.

Uterus

Little effect

Bronchi

Histamine release

Bronchoconstriction. Harmless normally, dangerous in asthmatics.

↓

↓liver

morphine-3-glucuronide: neuroexcitatory
↓
Kidney, some in bile — t½ 2–3 h · one dose acts 4–6 h · cleared in 24 h
Doses

5. Adverse effects

Sedation, Mental clouding, Lethargy, Dysphoria, Constipation, Vomiting, Respiratory depression, Blurred vision, Urinary retention, Fall in BP
Idiosyncrasy and allergy
Urticaria, lip swelling, itching from histamine release. Anaphylactoid reaction rare.
Apnoea of the newborn
Foetal blood-brain barrier is undeveloped → higher brain levels. Treat with naloxone 10 µg/kg into the umbilical cord.

Acute morphine poisoning

Serious toxicity
50 mg IM in a non-tolerant adult
Lethal dose
About 250 mg

Features

Stupor or coma, Flaccidity, Shallow breathing, Cyanosis, Pinpoint pupils, Fall in BP, Shock, Pulmonary oedema
Respiratory support: positive pressure breathing
also opposes pulmonary oedema
↓
Maintain BP: IV fluids, vasoconstrictors
↓
Gastric lavage with potassium permanganate
even if morphine was injected — it is a base, so it moves into acid gastric juice, ionises and can't diffuse back
↓
Antidote: naloxone 0.4–0.8 mg IV every 2–3 min until breathing picks up; repeat every 1–4 h

Tolerance and dependence

Tolerance

  • High degree; mainly cellular, partly faster metabolism
  • Strong cross-tolerance among opioids
  • Addicts tolerate grams

No tolerance to these

  • Constipation
  • Miosis
  • Convulsant action
Dependence
Pronounced, abuse liability high. Proper medical use seldom causes it; commoner among medical staff with easy access.

Withdrawal

Lacrimation, Sweating, Yawning, Anxiety, Restlessness, Gooseflesh, Mydriasis, Tremor, Insomnia, Abdominal colic, Diarrhoea, Dehydration, Rise in BP, Weight loss

Delirium and convulsions are rare, unlike barbiturate withdrawal. Antagonists precipitate it: 0.2 mg naloxone is enough in the severely dependent.

Withdraw morphine
↓
Substitute oral methadone
↓
Taper methadone
relapse is common: long-term maintenance or agonist-antagonists help

6. Precautions and contraindications

Head injury

Contraindicated

CO₂ retention raises intracranial tension · marked respiratory depression · vomiting, miosis and altered mentation hide how the injury is progressing

Bronchial asthma

Contraindicated

Histamine release can trigger an attack. If an opioid is unavoidable, use a low histamine releaser like fentanyl.

Respiratory insufficiency

Sudden deaths reported

Emphysema, pulmonary fibrosis, cor pulmonale. Worsens sleep apnoea.

Undiagnosed acute abdomen

Give after diagnosis

Worsens diverticulitis, biliary colic, pancreatitis; an inflamed appendix may rupture. Pentazocine and buprenorphine aggravate biliary spasm less.

More sensitive, use lower doses

Infants, Elderly, Hypotension, Hypovolaemia, Hypothyroidism, Liver disease, Kidney disease, Elderly males, Unstable personalities

Interactions

Potentiate opioids
Phenothiazines, Tricyclic antidepressants, MAO inhibitors, Amphetamine, Neostigmine
Sedative-hypnotics
Exaggerated CNS depression
Oral drugs
Morphine delays gastric emptying, so it slows their absorption
