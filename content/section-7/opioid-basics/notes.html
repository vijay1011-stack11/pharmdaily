Chapter 34 · Opioid analgesics and antagonists

Pain and the opioid receptors

What an analgesic is, how opioids are classified, and the three receptors that explain everything morphine does.

Two definitions

Pain (algesia)
Unpleasant bodily sensation felt as suffering. Set off by a noxious stimulus, outside or inside the body.
Analgesic
Relieves pain selectively — in the CNS or at peripheral pain mechanisms — without altering consciousness.

Opioid receptors

Three types — μ (mu), κ (kappa), δ (delta). All are G-protein coupled receptors. Morphine is an agonist at all three, but its affinity for μ is far higher, so its effects are μ effects.

μ · mu

Morphine's receptor

Analgesia · respiratory depression · sedation · euphoria · miosis · muscular rigidity · ↓ GI motility · dependence

Own ligands: endomorphin 1 and 2
μ₁ supraspinal analgesia · μ₂ spinal analgesia, respiratory depression, constipation
Dense in periaqueductal grey, thalamus, NTS, nucleus ambiguus, area postrema

κ · kappa

Dynorphin A

Analgesia (spinal κ₁) · respiratory depression, low ceiling · dysphoria, psychotomimetic · miosis · sedation · ↓ GI motility

Nalorphine-type dependence · antagonist: norbinaltorphimine
Some κ actions oppose μ actions

δ · delta

Enkephalins

Analgesia (mainly spinal) · respiratory depression · affective behaviour · reinforcement · ↓ GI motility · proconvulsant

Rich in limbic areas: the affective side of analgesia
Antagonist: naltrindole

σ · sigma

Not an opioid receptor

Morphine doesn't activate it · naloxone doesn't block it

Bound by pentazocine, butorphanol, phencyclidine
Explains naloxone-resistant dysphoria, hallucinations, tachycardia, mydriasis

Transducer mechanism

Receptors sit mostly on prejunctional neurones: less transmitter leaves cerebral, spinal and myenteric nerve endings, including primary pain afferents.

Endogenous opioid peptides

Morphine-like peptides from brain, pituitary, spinal cord and gut, found in the mid-1970s. Naloxone blocks them. Three families, each cut from its own large precursor.

What the system modulates

Pain perception, Mood, Pleasure, Motor behaviour, Emesis, Pituitary hormones, Gut motility

Injected into the brain it is 20–40× more potent than morphine. Enkephalins and dynorphins have a short t½ and wide spread: they act as neuromodulators or transmitters.

Naloxone blocks all three

Placebo analgesia, Acupuncture analgesia, Stress-induced analgesia

So endogenous opioids drive them. Nociceptin / orphanin FQ acts on the NOP receptor: opioid-like or anti-opioid depending on the site.
