Chapter 29 · Benzodiazepines

Benzodiazepines

Selective CNS depressants: they sedate, relieve anxiety, help sleep, stop seizures and relax muscle. The most used group, and the safest.

Benzodiazepines have a lower ceiling of CNS depression than barbiturates and a high therapeutic index. Even 10 hypnotic doses taken together don't usually endanger life: the patient can be roused and breathing rarely needs support.

Benzodiazepines bind specific subunits of the GABA-A receptor, which is why one group gives sedative-hypnotic, anticonvulsant, antianxiety and muscle relaxant actions.

Inside the receptor

No GABA-mimetic action
Benzodiazepines don't open the Cl⁻ channel by themselves; they only help GABA. That explains their lower ceiling.
α1 subunit
Sedative, hypnotic, amnesic actions. Z compounds act selectively here.
α2 subunit
Anxiolytic and muscle relaxant actions. Diazepam acts on α1, α2, α3 and α5: broad spectrum.
Where in the brain
Midbrain reticular formation (wakefulness) and limbic system. Muscle relaxation: medulla. Ataxia: cerebellum.

What this does: actions

Antianxiety
Relatively selective; not just a side effect of sedation. Drowsiness wears off with regular use, but anxiety relief stays.
Muscle relaxant
Centrally acting, without impairing voluntary activity. Clonazepam and diazepam are strongest.
Anticonvulsant
Clonazepam, diazepam, nitrazepam, lorazepam and flurazepam are strongest. Tolerance limits long-term use in epilepsy.
Other
IV diazepam causes some analgesia (barbiturates cause hyperalgesia). Diazepam lowers night-time gastric acid and prevents stress ulcers.

3. Therapeutic uses

1. Dental sedationConscious sedation: relieves anxiety and pain. Small sedative doses alter consciousness, but the patient keeps a patent airway and responds to verbal commands.
Intravenous
Midazolam, Diazepam
Oral
Midazolam, Diazepam, Nitrazepam
Inhalation
Nitrous oxide
2. BruxismGrinding of the teeth, usually with jaw clenching. Causes permanent tooth damage, TMJ dysfunction and muscle pain, and severe headache.
Drug used: diazepam. Others: buspirone, gabapentin, propranolol, botulinum toxin, cyclobenzaprine (muscle relaxant).
3. Insomnia
Shorter sleep latency, fewer awakenings, longer total sleep
4. Anxiety
Acute stress disorder, generalised anxiety disorder (GAD), panic disorder, phobic disorder. Alprazolam also has antidepressant action.
5. IV anaesthetic
Diazepam and midazolam for induction in adults; slow onset and recovery
6. Preanaesthetic medication
Sedative, antianxiety and amnesic actions before surgery
7. Anticonvulsant
Absence and myoclonic seizures, infantile spasms: clonazepam, clobazam (tolerance limits long-term use) · Status epilepticus: IV lorazepam, IV or rectal diazepam · Febrile convulsions: IV or rectal diazepam · Drug overdose convulsions · Organophosphate nerve agents (tabun, sarin, soman) · Tetanus
8. Muscle relaxant
Muscle spasm; spastic disorders: multiple sclerosis, cerebral palsy, head injury, stroke
9. Pre-procedure sedation
ECT, cardioversion, cardiac catheterisation, angiography, endoscopy
10. Alcohol withdrawal
Controls the acute symptoms
11. Adjuvant
Stress-induced peptic ulcer; anticipatory emesis

They shorten each REM phase but allow more REM cycles, so total REM falls less than with barbiturates. Some tolerance to the sleep effect develops with nightly use.

4. Adverse effects

Drug dependence
Stopping suddenly after long use causes withdrawal: rebound insomnia, dysphoria, irritability, bad dreams, tremors, restlessness, anxiety. Taper the dose when stopping.
CNS
Drowsiness, light-headedness, anterograde amnesia, lassitude, hangover, confusion. Impaired judgement, motor skills and cognition: patients shouldn't drive.
Other
Muscle weakness, blurred vision, vertigo, ataxia
Also
Sleep walking and sleep driving with no memory of it · Paradoxical stimulation, nightmares, sweating (esp. flurazepam) · Elderly more prone to psychomotor effects · Aggravate sleep apnoea · Dry mouth, urinary incontinence
Pregnancy
Floppy infant syndrome: given in labour, benzodiazepines cause flaccidity and respiratory depression in the newborn. Flumazenil reverses it.
Cleft lip and palate: reported with first-trimester use.
Abuse
Low dependence liability: weak reinforcers, seldom abused alone. Withdrawal usually mild; worse with fast-eliminated drugs. Convulsions rare.

Drug interactions

Other CNS depressants
Alcohol and other CNS depressants add up: excessive impairment
Sodium valproate
Together they have caused psychotic symptoms
CYP3A4 inhibitors
Ketoconazole, erythromycin prolong benzodiazepine action. Cimetidine, isoniazid and oral contraceptives also slow benzodiazepine metabolism.
For your clinicA patient on erythromycin given oral midazolam for sedation stays drowsy much longer: erythromycin slows its breakdown by CYP3A4.

5. Antidote: flumazenil

A benzodiazepine analogue with almost no action of its own. It competes with benzodiazepines (and inverse agonists) at the benzodiazepine receptor and reverses their effects. It also blocks zolpidem-like Z compounds.
Route
Intravenous. Acts in seconds, lasts 1–2 hours; t½ about 1 hour.
Reverse benzodiazepine anaesthesia
Patient wakes and regains control within a minute of 0.3–1 mg IV. Re-sedation may come within an hour.
Benzodiazepine overdose
0.2 mg/min IV until conscious, max 1 mg; most respond within 5 min. Supportive care alone works for most.
Hepatic encephalopathy
Used to reverse encephalopathy in liver failure
Caution
Risky in mixed overdose with a tricyclic antidepressant, and in benzodiazepine-dependent people: can trigger severe withdrawal and seizures
Side effects
Agitation, discomfort, fear, anxiety, coldness, withdrawal seizures
For your clinicKeep flumazenil ready whenever you give IV midazolam or diazepam for dental sedation. It reverses over-sedation in about a minute.

More to know: advantages over barbiturates

Safety
Wide therapeutic index; risk of fatal CNS depression is minimal
Sleep quality
Deep, refreshing sleep; less distortion of sleep stages; less rebound on stopping
Breathing and heart
Don't affect respiratory or cardiovascular function at hypnotic doses. Higher doses cause mild respiratory depression and low BP, which matters only in lung or heart disease. IV injection lowers BP.
Enzymes
Don't induce liver microsomal enzymes, so no enzyme-induction interactions
Abuse
Lower abuse liability; tolerance mild; dependence and withdrawal less marked
Antidote
Specific antagonist flumazenil is available

Pharmacokinetics

  • Lipid solubility differs more than 50-fold between benzodiazepines, which drives most of the differences
  • IM absorption is irregular, except lorazepam
  • Plasma protein binding: flurazepam 10% to diazepam 99%
  • Lipid-soluble ones enter brain fast, then redistribute: a single dose can act briefly even with a long t½
  • Metabolised in liver by CYP3A4 and CYP2C19; many have active metabolites, so the real effect outlasts plasma t½
  • Diazepam undergoes enterohepatic circulation; most are excreted in urine as glucuronides
  • Cross the placenta and enter milk

Individual drugs

Diazepam
Oldest, all-purpose benzodiazepine. Active metabolites (desmethyldiazepam, oxazepam). Less rebound insomnia on stopping.
Flurazepam
Very long t½: next-morning sedation, builds up over 3–5 days. Not for the elderly.
Nitrazepam
Equipotent to diazepam. For frequent night awakenings when some daytime sedation is acceptable.
Lorazepam
No active metabolite, so no build-up. Mainly for anxiety; also sleep in tense patients.
Alprazolam
Mainly for anxiety; also a night-time hypnotic. Rapid absorption, short t½, few next-day effects.
Triazolam
Very potent, peaks in under an hour. Early-morning waking with anxiety; anterograde amnesia. Withdrawn in the UK.
