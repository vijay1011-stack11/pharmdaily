Prilocaine

  • Like lignocaine, but no vasodilatation at the site
  • Lower CNS toxicity: larger volume of distribution, faster clearance
  • Methaemoglobinaemia: aromatic ring metabolised to o-toluidine
  • Infiltration, nerve block, spinal, IV regional

EMLA: lignocaine + prilocaine

Lignocaine 2.5% + prilocaine 2.5%, equal parts, 25°C
↓eutectic: melting point drops
Oil → emulsified into a cream
↓
Apply under occlusive dressing for 1 h
↓
Numb to 5 mm depth · lasts 1–2 h after removal
  • The only LA that anaesthetises intact skin
  • Before IV cannulation, split-skin graft harvesting, superficial procedures
  • Alternative to lignocaine infiltration

Ropivacaine

  • Newer bupivacaine congener: nearly as long-acting, less cardiotoxic
  • Blocks Aδ and C (pain) more than Aβ (motor)
  • Lower lipid solubility → enters large motor fibres less → lower CNS and cardiac toxicity
  • S(−) enantiomer · needs slightly higher concentrations than bupivacaine
  • Best sensory–motor separation in epidurals → post-op and labour pain
  • Less cardiotoxic → preferred for large-volume nerve blocks · 0.2% injection

Cocaine

Source
Natural alkaloid, leaves of Erythroxylon coca, a South American plant · chewed as a stimulant in Peru and Bolivia
History
First LA: ocular anaesthesia, 1884
Actions
Surface anaesthetic + strong CNS stimulant
Status now
No longer used medically · illicit market only · never injected: protoplasmic poison, tissue necrosis

Surface-only drugs

Tetracaine

PABA ester · amethocaine

Highly lipid-soluble, more potent and toxic: slow hydrolysis by pseudocholinesterase. Topical use limited to eye, nose, throat.

Not for bronchoscopy: tracheobronchial spray absorbs almost like IV.

Proparacaine

Commonest eye anaesthetic

Meta-aminobenzoic acid derivative. Faster than tetracaine, equally effective. Tonometry 30 s after one 0.5% drop. Cornea numb 10–20 min.

Cataract: 1–2 drops every 5–10 min, max 5. Foreign body or suture removal: 2–3 applications. Minimal irritation, few allergies.

Dibucaine

Most potent, toxic, longest

Cinchocaine. Surface use on less delicate mucosa or skin: ear canal, anal canal (1.1% ear drops). Spinal use abandoned.

Oxethazaine

Works in stomach acid

Ionises very little even at low pH → anaesthetises gastric mucosa. With antacids for gastritis, drug-induced gastric irritation, reflux, heartburn of pregnancy.

0.2% suspension, 5–10 ml orally · >100 mg/day: dizziness, drowsiness

More LAs from college notes

Mepivacaine

Amide · injectable

Similar to lignocaine in onset and duration, but less vasodilatation, so it can be used without a vasoconstrictor (3% plain in dentistry). Nerve blocks.

Articaine

Amide · dental

Amide with a thiophene ring and an ester side chain, so it is partly broken down in plasma and has a short half-life. Dental 4% with adrenaline.

Etidocaine

Amide · long-acting

Long-acting, highly lipid-soluble amide. Produces marked motor block relative to sensory block.

Benoxinate

Ester · surface, eye

Oxybuprocaine. Eye drops (0.4%) for tonometry and minor eye procedures.

Dyclonine

Surface · mucosa

Topical anaesthetic for mouth and throat: lozenges, sprays. Chemically a ketone, not an ester or amide.
