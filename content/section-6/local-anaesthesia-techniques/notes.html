1. Surface anaesthesia

  • Topical, on mucous membranes or abraded skin
  • Only the superficial layer · no motor loss
  • Lidocaine 10% throat spray: onset 2–5 min, lasts 30–45 min
  • Adrenaline doesn't prolong it; phenylephrine does (mucosal vasoconstriction)
  • Soluble LAs absorb fast from mucosa · tetracaine throat spray ≈ IV blood levels
  • Only EMLA works on intact skin

  • Oral mucositis pain after radiation or chemotherapy
  • Postherpetic neuralgia, cold sores

2. Infiltration anaesthesia

  • Dilute LA injected into and around the operation site → nerve endings blocked
  • No individual nerves identified
  • Onset almost immediate
  • Shorter than nerve block: lidocaine 30–60 min, bupivacaine 90–180 min
  • Minor operations: incisions, excisions, suturing, hydrocele, herniorrhaphy
  • Dental tooth extraction, root canal treatment, periodontal procedures
  • Drugs: lidocaine, bupivacaine, procaine · with adrenaline
  • Needs a lot of drug for the area covered · motor function spared

3. Conduction block

  • LA around nerve trunks → area distal to the injection is anaesthetised and paralysed
  • Lidocaine 1–2% usual · bupivacaine for longer blocks

Lower limbs, pelvis, lower abdomen: prostatectomy, fracture setting, obstetric procedures, caesarean

Advantages over GA

  • Safer
  • Analgesia + muscle relaxation, patient conscious
  • Fewer problems in cardiac, lung, kidney disease and diabetes

Contraindications

  • Hypotension, hypovolaemia
  • Uncooperative or mentally ill
  • Infants and children
  • Bleeding diathesis
  • Raised intracranial pressure
  • Vertebral deformity (kyphosis, lordosis)
  • Sepsis at injection site

Complications

1. Respiratory paralysis
Rare; diaphragm (phrenic) keeps breathing · hypotension and respiratory-centre ischaemia cause failure more often · weak cough → lung complications
2. Hypotension
Sympathetic block → venodilatation, venous pooling, ↓ venous return → ↓ cardiac output (more than arteriolar dilatation) · leg paralysis adds
Fix: raise foot end; ephedrine, mephentermine
Bradycardia, heart block
↓ Sympathetic flow to the heart + low venous return
3. Neurological symptoms
Brief back and leg pain or paraesthesia, more with lidocaine · no deficit
4. Headache
CSF leakage → use a smaller-bore needle
Backache
College notes
5. Cauda equina syndrome
Very rare: urinary retention and incontinence, faecal incontinence, impotence · root trauma, antiseptic arachnoiditis, LA neurotoxicity
Septic meningitis
College notes
6. Nausea, vomiting
Traction on abdominal viscera · prevent with ondansetron or metoclopramide

5. Epidural anaesthesia

  • Injected into the epidural space: lumbar, sacral (caudal), thoracic or cervical
  • Obstetric and surgical procedures · bupivacaine, lidocaine, ropivacaine, with adrenaline
  • RCOG: bupivacaine is the gold standard for obstetric epidural analgesia
  • Epidural space: semiliquid fat, nerve roots pass through
  • Acts on nerve roots (epidural + diffuses subarachnoid); some leaks out for paravertebral blocks

(i) Thoracic

Narrow space, small volume

Wide band of mid and lower thoracic analgesia. Pain after thoracic or upper abdominal surgery; catheter for days.

(ii) Lumbar

Wide space, large volume

Lower abdomen, pelvis, legs. Uses like spinal.

(iii) Caudal

Through the sacral hiatus

Pelvis and perineum. Vaginal delivery, anorectal and genitourinary operations.

Exam case · KDT problem-directed study
  • Healthy primigravida, 26, in labour, no contraindication, wants pain relief
  • Answer: lumbar epidural with 0.25% bupivacaine (or ropivacaine)
  • Why: sensory block without motor block, so she can push; less drug reaches the foetus
