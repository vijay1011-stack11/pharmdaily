Chapter 34 · Opioid analgesics and antagonists

Pain and the opioid receptors

What an analgesic is, how opioids are classified, and the three receptors that explain everything morphine does.

Two definitions

Pain (algesia)
Unpleasant bodily sensation felt as suffering. Set off by a noxious stimulus, outside or inside the body.
Analgesic
Relieves pain selectively — in the CNS or at peripheral pain mechanisms — without altering consciousness.

Opioid receptors

Three types — μ (mu), κ (kappa), δ (delta). All are G-protein coupled receptors. Morphine is an agonist at all three, but its affinity for μ is far higher, so its effects are μ effects.

μ · mu

Morphine's receptor

Analgesia · respiratory depression · sedation · euphoria · miosis · muscular rigidity · ↓ GI motility · dependence

Own ligands: endomorphin 1 and 2
μ₁ supraspinal analgesia · μ₂ spinal analgesia, respiratory depression, constipation
Dense in periaqueductal grey, thalamus, NTS, nucleus ambiguus, area postrema

κ · kappa

Dynorphin A

Analgesia (spinal κ₁) · respiratory depression, low ceiling · dysphoria, psychotomimetic · miosis · sedation · ↓ GI motility

Nalorphine-type dependence · antagonist: norbinaltorphimine
Some κ actions oppose μ actions

δ · delta

Enkephalins

Analgesia (mainly spinal) · respiratory depression · affective behaviour · reinforcement · ↓ GI motility · proconvulsant

Rich in limbic areas: the affective side of analgesia
Antagonist: naltrindole

σ · sigma

Not an opioid receptor

Morphine doesn't activate it · naloxone doesn't block it

Bound by pentazocine, butorphanol, phencyclidine
Explains naloxone-resistant dysphoria, hallucinations, tachycardia, mydriasis

Transducer mechanism

Receptors sit mostly on prejunctional neurones: less transmitter leaves cerebral, spinal and myenteric nerve endings, including primary pain afferents.

Endogenous opioid peptides

Morphine-like peptides from brain, pituitary, spinal cord and gut, found in the mid-1970s. Naloxone blocks them. Three families, each cut from its own large precursor.

Large precursor
POMClimited spread
  • β-Endorphin31 amino acids
  • Selectivity μ > δ >> κ
  • Arcuate nucleus, anterior pituitary, pancreatic islets
  • Also gives γ-MSH, ACTH, lipotropins
Proenkephalinwide spread
  • Met- and leu-enkephalinpentapeptides
  • Selectivity δ ≥ μ >> κ
  • Pain areas, limbic system, locus coeruleus, adrenal medulla, gut glands
Prodynorphinwide spread
  • Dynorphin A and B8–17 amino acids
  • Selectivity κ >> μ = δ
  • Parallel to proenkephalin, but different neurones

What the system modulates

Pain perception, Mood, Pleasure, Motor behaviour, Emesis, Pituitary hormones, Gut motility

β-Endorphin as a neurohormone

LH, FSH↓
GH, prolactin↑

Injected into the brain it is 20–40× more potent than morphine. Enkephalins and dynorphins have a short t½ and wide spread: they act as neuromodulators or transmitters.

Naloxone blocks all three

Placebo analgesia, Acupuncture analgesia, Stress-induced analgesia

So endogenous opioids drive them. Nociceptin / orphanin FQ acts on the NOP receptor: opioid-like or anti-opioid depending on the site.

Chapter 34 · Morphine

Morphine, the prototype

The main alkaloid in opium and still widely used. A full μ agonist: know its actions and the rest of the chapter is comparisons.

1. CNS actions

Depressant actions: what morphine switches down

Morphine → μ receptors across the CNS
↓each centre it depresses gives one effect
(a) Pain pathway → analgesia
Dull, poorly localised visceral pain relieved better than sharp somatic pain; neuritic pain responds less. Relieves the perception of pain and its emotional part, so the patient tolerates it better. Other sensations stay intact.
↓
(b) Cortex, reticular formation → sedation
Drowsiness, mental dullness, indifference, but no ataxia (unlike hypnotics). No anticonvulsant action: fits may be precipitated.
↓
(c⁠) Limbic, reward pathway → euphoria
Calming, detachment, heavy warm limbs. Unpleasant in normal people without pain; a "high" in pain, anxiety or addicts.
↓
(d) Respiratory centre → respiratory depression
Rate and tidal volume both fall. Marked with other sedatives, or in asthma, COPD, cor pulmonale, liver and kidney disease. Death in poisoning is from respiratory failure.
↓
(e) Cough centre → cough suppression
More sensitive to morphine than the respiratory centre
↓
(f) Hypothalamic thermostat → hypothermia
In cold surroundings
↓
(g) Vasomotor centre → fall in BP
Depressed at higher doses

How the analgesia works

SpinalSubstantia gelatinosa of dorsal horn↓↓ glutamate, substance P↓Pain "gate" closesintrathecal morphine: segmental analgesia
SupraspinalMedulla, periaqueductal grey, limbic, cortex↓Alters pain processing↓Boosts descending inhibition5-HT and NA pathways to the cord

The two sites multiply each other. Morphine also acts on peripheral afferent terminals in injured tissue.

Why it feels good, and why it's addictive

μ agonist inhibits GABAergic neurones in the nucleus accumbens
↓
↑ Dopamine release
↓
Euphoria and reinforcement

κ agonists do the opposite: ↓ dopamine, aversion. Less NA release in the locus coeruleus relieves fear and apprehension.

Stimulant actions: what it switches up

CTZ → nausea and vomiting
Worse if the stomach is full or the patient walks about. Larger doses depress the vomiting centre, so emetics don't work in morphine poisoning.
↓
Edinger-Westphal nucleus → miosis
Removes GABAergic inhibition of the III nerve nucleus. No tolerance: addicts keep constricted pupils.
↓
Vagal centre → bradycardia
↓
Cortex and hippocampus → truncal rigidity, convulsions
Rigidity at high IV doses can hamper breathing.

(a) CVS

Vasodilatation comes from histamine release, vasomotor depression and a direct action on vessels; blood shifts from the pulmonary to the systemic circuit. Rate may rise reflexly if BP drops.

(b) GIT: constipation

(c⁠) Other smooth muscle

Biliary

Intrabiliary pressure ↑ several fold → biliary colic. Countered by naloxone and nitrates.

Urinary

Uterus

Little effect

Bronchi

Bronchoconstriction. Harmless normally, dangerous in asthmatics.

5. Adverse effects

Sedation, Mental clouding, Lethargy, Dysphoria, Constipation, Vomiting, Respiratory depression, Blurred vision, Urinary retention, Fall in BP
Idiosyncrasy and allergy
Urticaria, lip swelling, itching from histamine release. Anaphylactoid reaction rare.
Apnoea of the newborn
Foetal blood-brain barrier is undeveloped → higher brain levels. Treat with naloxone 10 µg/kg into the umbilical cord.

Acute morphine poisoning

Serious toxicity
50 mg IM in a non-tolerant adult
Lethal dose
About 250 mg

Features

Stupor or coma, Flaccidity, Shallow breathing, Cyanosis, Pinpoint pupils, Fall in BP, Shock, Pulmonary oedema
Respiratory support: positive pressure breathing
also opposes pulmonary oedema
↓
Maintain BP: IV fluids, vasoconstrictors
↓
Gastric lavage with potassium permanganate
even if morphine was injected — it is a base, so it moves into acid gastric juice, ionises and can't diffuse back
↓
Antidote: naloxone 0.4–0.8 mg IV every 2–3 min until breathing picks up; repeat every 1–4 h

Tolerance and dependence

Tolerance

  • High degree; mainly cellular, partly faster metabolism
  • Strong cross-tolerance among opioids
  • Addicts tolerate grams

No tolerance to these

  • Constipation
  • Miosis
  • Convulsant action
Dependence
Pronounced, abuse liability high. Proper medical use seldom causes it; commoner among medical staff with easy access.

Withdrawal

Lacrimation, Sweating, Yawning, Anxiety, Restlessness, Gooseflesh, Mydriasis, Tremor, Insomnia, Abdominal colic, Diarrhoea, Dehydration, Rise in BP, Weight loss

Delirium and convulsions are rare, unlike barbiturate withdrawal. Antagonists precipitate it: 0.2 mg naloxone is enough in the severely dependent.

Withdraw morphine
↓
Substitute oral methadone
↓
Taper methadone
relapse is common: long-term maintenance or agonist-antagonists help

6. Precautions and contraindications

Head injury

Contraindicated

CO₂ retention raises intracranial tension · marked respiratory depression · vomiting, miosis and altered mentation hide how the injury is progressing

Bronchial asthma

Contraindicated

Histamine release can trigger an attack. If an opioid is unavoidable, use a low histamine releaser like fentanyl.

Respiratory insufficiency

Sudden deaths reported

Emphysema, pulmonary fibrosis, cor pulmonale. Worsens sleep apnoea.

Undiagnosed acute abdomen

Give after diagnosis

Worsens diverticulitis, biliary colic, pancreatitis; an inflamed appendix may rupture. Pentazocine and buprenorphine aggravate biliary spasm less.

More sensitive, use lower doses

Infants, Elderly, Hypotension, Hypovolaemia, Hypothyroidism, Liver disease, Kidney disease, Elderly males, Unstable personalities

Interactions

Potentiate opioids
Phenothiazines, Tricyclic antidepressants, MAO inhibitors, Amphetamine, Neostigmine
Sedative-hypnotics
Exaggerated CNS depression
Oral drugs
Morphine delays gastric emptying, so it slows their absorption

7. Uses

Uses
1. Analgesicthe main use
  • Severe paintrauma, visceral, ischaemic (MI), postoperative, burns, cancer, renal colic
  • Cancer and terminal painoral continuous-release morphine, fentanyl patch, sublingual buprenorphine · round-the-clock dosing + a rescue dose
  • Epidural 2–3 mg, intrathecal 0.2 mgsegmental analgesia ~12 h; respiratory depression can appear late
  • Patient-controlled analgesiapatient sets the IV fentanyl rate
  • Not for neuropathic painpregabalin, amitriptyline, duloxetine instead
2–3. Anaesthesiabefore and during
  • Preanaesthetic medicationmorphine or pethidine, selected patients
  • Balanced anaesthesiafentanyl, remifentanil or morphine
4–5. CardiacIV morphine
  • Acute LVF, cardiac asthma
  • Anxiety and apprehensionin MI or internal bleeding — never as a routine anxiolytic or sleeping drug
6–7. Codeine's jobsnot morphine
  • Dry, irritating cough
  • Diarrhoealoperamide and diphenoxylate are the dedicated antidiarrhoeals

Analgesia is symptomatic: don't relieve pain before it has been assessed, it helps diagnosis. In MI give promptly, to cut apprehension and reflex sympathetic stimulation.

Why morphine helps acute LVF

IV morphine
↓
Venodilatation, peripheral pooling → ↓ preload
↓
Blood shifts out of the lungs
↓
Respiratory centre depressed → air hunger relieved
↓
Sympathetic drive and cardiac work ↓

Also for pulmonary oedema from lung infarction, but not from irritant gases. Contraindicated in bronchial asthma.

For your clinicToothache: start with an aspirin-like analgesic. If that isn't enough, add oral codeine, tramadol or tapentadol, alone or with paracetamol. Adding an NSAID lets you keep the opioid dose low.
For your clinicA patient with a facial fracture and head injury: morphine is contraindicated. It raises intracranial tension and masks the pupil and consciousness changes you need to watch.

Ceiling = analgesic efficacy, + low to ++++ full. The dose given is the equianalgesic dose.

1. Codeine

Methyl-morphine: a natural opium alkaloid and a partial μ agonist with a low ceiling. It has very little affinity for opioid receptors itself.
Normal metaboliserCodeine↓CYP2D6 demethylation↓Morphine↓Analgesia
CYP2D6 poor metaboliserCodeine↓Little conversion↓No morphine↓Analgesia fails
Analgesia
1/10 of morphine · 60 mg codeine ≈ 600 mg aspirin · mild to moderate pain only
Cough
1/3 of morphine, more selective · subanalgesic 10–30 mg
Kinetics
Oral : parenteral 1 : 2 · acts 4–6 h · no parenteral preparation
Side effects
Constipation prominent, so it is used for diarrhoea. Others milder than morphine; abuse liability low.

Pholcodine, ethylmorphine

Codeine-like

Used mainly as antitussives; said to be less constipating.

Heroin

3× morphine

More lipid-soluble → reaches the brain faster → more euphoric and highly addicting. No therapeutic advantage; banned in most countries.

2. Pethidine (meperidine)

Made in 1939 as an atropine substitute. Chemically unlike morphine, but a μ agonist blocked by naloxone.

Morphine10 mg↓Acts 4–6 h↓Suppresses cough↓Bradycardiamarked miosis and constipation
Pethidine~100 mg↓Acts 2–4 h↓No cough relief↓Tachycardiaantimuscarinic: dry mouth, blurred vision; less miosis, constipation, retention

Why overdose excites instead of depressing

Pethidine
↓liver
Meperidinic acid + a little norpethidine
↓builds up in renal failure, or with MAO inhibitors
Tremors, mydriasis, hyperreflexia, delirium, myoclonus, convulsions
Interactions
Nonselective MAO inhibitors: norpethidine builds up, excitement · SSRIs: serotonin syndrome
Use now
Declined. Controls shivering after anaesthesia or during IV infusions. Dose 50–100 mg IM or SC.

3. Fentanyl and remifentanil

Fentanyl

80–100× morphine

Pethidine congener. Few cardiovascular effects, less histamine release.

Very lipid-soluble: peak 5 min after IV, gone in 30–40 min by redistribution; elimination t½ ~4 h.
Injection for anaesthesia · transdermal patch for cancer and chronic pain, working after ~12 h, changed every 3 days · buccal possible, not oral.

Remifentanil

Fastest

Faster-acting fentanyl congener, lasting only 10–15 min after IV. Used only in anaesthesia.

4. Methadone

Synthetic, chemically unlike morphine but pharmacologically very similar. Its two key features: high oral activity and firm tissue binding.
Single dose acts 4–6 h
↓accumulates in tissues, released slowly
Repeated doses act longer — t½ 24–48 h on chronic use
↓
Hardly any "kick" · slow tolerance · withdrawal starts in 1–2 days, prolonged but milder
Substitution therapy
1 mg methadone replaces 4 mg morphine, 2 mg heroin or 20 mg pethidine, then is withdrawn gradually
Methadone maintenance
40–80 mg/day long-term, so injected morphine or heroin gives no pleasure
Kinetics
Oral : parenteral 1 : 2 · 90% protein bound · CYP3A4 and CYP2B6
Interactions
Rifampicin and phenytoin induce its metabolism → withdrawal in methadone-dependent patients
Other uses
Analgesic 2.5–10 mg oral or IM; occasionally antitussive

Dextropropoxyphene, a weak opioid related to methadone, was banned in India in 2013 after overdoses caused delirium, seizures and cardiotoxicity.

5. Tramadol

Centrally acting atypical opioid. Naloxone only partly reverses it: part of its action is non-opioid (monoamine reuptake block).
Action 1Weak μ agonist↓Opioid analgesianaloxone-reversible part
Action 2↓ Reuptake of NA and 5-HT, ↑ 5-HT release↓Spinal monoaminergic pain inhibitionnaloxone-resistant part

Advantages

  • Insignificant respiratory depression, sedation, constipation, urinary retention
  • Good oral bioavailability; t½ 5–6 h
  • Minimal haemodynamic effects
  • Little dose escalation; low abuse potential

Watch for

  • Nausea and dizziness prominent
  • Lowers seizure threshold: contraindicated in epilepsy
  • With SSRIs: serotonin syndrome
  • Not effective in severe pain
Potency
100 mg IV tramadol ≈ 10 mg IM morphine
Uses
Mild to moderate short-lasting pain: diagnostic procedures, injury, surgery. Also chronic and cancer pain. 50–100 mg oral, IM or slow IV, 4–6 hourly; also with paracetamol.

6. Tapentadol

TramadolWeak μ agonist+NA and 5-HT reuptake blockedmore nausea and vomiting
TapentadolWeak μ binding+NA reuptake blocked > 5-HTmainly noradrenergic; less nausea
Kinetics
Well absorbed orally · glucuronide conjugated · excreted in urine
Use
Moderately severe acute and chronic pain; at least as effective as tramadol. 50–100 mg, 2–4 times a day.
Still risks
Serotonin syndrome with SSRIs · seizures in predisposed patients
For your clinicBefore prescribing tramadol or tapentadol after an extraction, ask about antidepressants (SSRIs) and epilepsy. Both are reasons to pick another analgesic.
Chapter 34 · Complex action opioids

Complex action opioids

Drugs that switch one receptor on and another off: limited efficacy, a ceiling on respiratory depression, less addicting.

Agonist-antagonists are agonists at one opioid receptor and antagonists at another. Partial agonists bind well but have low intrinsic activity.

Where each drug sits

Morphine, pethidine, fentanylfull μ agonist
Buprenorphinepartial μ agonist + κ antagonist
Butorphanolweak μ partial agonist + κ agonist
Pentazocine, nalbuphine, nalorphineμ antagonist + κ agonist
Naloxone, naltrexone, nalmefenepure antagonist, no agonist action

Intrinsic activity at the μ receptor, highest at the top. Everything below the first band has a ceiling.

Shared features

  • Limited efficacy: like lower doses of morphine
  • Low-ceiling respiratory depression
  • Less addicting

Don't give

  • After a full μ agonist: analgesia may fall
  • To someone dependent on a μ agonist: severe withdrawal may be precipitated
Nalorphine
N-allyl-normorphine, the first opioid antagonist (1951). Later found to be a κ agonist with low-ceiling analgesia. Not used: dysphoric and psychotomimetic.

Pentazocine

The first agonist-antagonist analgesic: weak μ antagonist, marked κ agonist.

Dose decides how it feels

Low dose — pleasurable; post-addicts recognise it as an opiate
↓above 60 mg IM
Unpleasant, nalorphine-like, psychotomimetic
κ and σ mediated

CVS: why it is wrong for MI

Sympathetic stimulation at higher doses↑
Heart rate↑
Blood pressure↑
Cardiac work↑

Avoid in coronary ischaemia and MI — the opposite of morphine, which cuts cardiac work.

Analgesia
Lower ceiling, mainly spinal (κ₁), different in character from morphine's
Sedation, respiration
1/3 to 1/2 of morphine · doesn't increase much beyond 60 mg
Better than morphine
Less biliary spasm, constipation and vomiting. Sweating and light-headedness instead.
Dependence
Occurs, but withdrawal is milder and drug-seeking less intense. Precipitates withdrawal in morphine-dependent people.
Kinetics and dose
Oral : parenteral 1 : 3 · t½ 3–4 h, acts 3–5 h · oral 50–100 mg, IM or SC 30–60 mg (irritant, causes local fibrosis)
Use
Not favoured now. Postoperative and moderately severe pain; dysphoria limits chronic use.

Butorphanol and nalbuphine

Butorphanolκ agonist, weak μ partial agonist↓2 mg = 30 mg pentazocine↓More sedation↓Avoid in cardiac ischaemiaBP not raised
NalbuphineStrong κ agonist, weak μ antagonist↓More potent than pentazocine↓No sympathetic stimulation↓Safe in MIan analogue of naloxone

Butorphanol

1–4 mg IM or IV

Less dysphoric than pentazocine; post-addicts recognise it as a barbiturate. Low abuse potential, mild withdrawal.

Can neither substitute for nor antagonise morphine. For postoperative pain and renal colic.

Nalbuphine

Ceiling at 30 mg IM

No further analgesia or respiratory depression above it. Few dysphoric effects at 10–20 mg; higher doses give weird thoughts and hallucinations.

t½ 2–3 h · 10 mg IM, SC or IV every 4–6 h · precipitates withdrawal in morphine-dependent people

Buprenorphine

Synthetic thebaine congener. A highly lipid-soluble partial μ agonist, 25× more potent than morphine but with lower intrinsic activity and a ceiling. Also a κ antagonist.

Why naloxone struggles with it

Buprenorphine binds μ receptors very tightly
↓
Naloxone can't displace it once bound
↓
Only partly reversed — and not usable in labour: neonatal respiratory depression can't be reversed

High-dose naloxone given first can prevent its effect. Naloxone doesn't precipitate buprenorphine withdrawal.

Time course
Slower onset, longer action: 6–8 h after one dose, up to 24 h on repeat dosing
Effects
Sedation, vomiting, miosis like morphine · less constipation · postural hypotension prominent · analgesia and respiratory depression both show a ceiling
Dependence
Substitutes for morphine at low dependence, but precipitates withdrawal in the highly dependent. Its own withdrawal is delayed by days, milder and longer.
Kinetics
Effective sublingually · highly protein bound, stays in tissues for days · terminal t½ ~40 h · excreted mostly unchanged in bile
Dose
0.3–0.6 mg IM, SC or slow IV · sublingual 0.2–0.4 mg every 6–8 h
Uses
Long-lasting cancer pain, premedication, postoperative pain, MI, and opioid dependence as an alternative to methadone
Chapter 34 · Opioid antagonists

Pure opioid antagonists

Drugs with no agonist action of their own: they block opioid receptors and reverse opioid poisoning.

Naloxone, Naltrexone, Nalmefene

Naloxone

N-allyl-noroxymorphone. A competitive antagonist at all opioid receptors, blocking μ at much lower doses than κ or δ. No agonist action even at 20× the μ-blocking dose.
No opioid on boardNaloxone↓Nothing happensno subjective effects, no dependence
Opioid on boardNaloxone↓Every opioid effect reversedwithin 2–3 min of IV
Naloxone 0.4–0.8 mg IV in a morphine-treated patient
↓within 2–3 min
Analgesia gone · breathing normal, even stimulated · pupils dilate
↓
Sedation reverses less completely
↓effect lasts only 1–3 h
The opioid may outlast it: repeat the dose

Dose ladder

0.4–0.8 mg IVreverses morphine
0.4 mg IVprecipitates withdrawal in dependence, lasting 2–3 h
4–10 mgreverses nalorphine and pentazocine
No dose worksbuprenorphine

The dysphoric and psychotomimetic effects of pentazocine-like drugs are only partly reversed, because they are σ mediated.

Endogenous opioids
Blocked too: people with high pain tolerance become more pain-sensitive. Partly reverses respiratory depression from N₂O and diazepam.
Kinetics
Inactive orally (first pass) · IV acts in 2–3 min · glucuronidated · t½ 1 h in adults, 3 h in newborns · lasts 1–3 h
Adverse effects
Uncommon: rise in BP, pulmonary oedema

Uses of naloxone

Naloxone
Emergencyreverse the opioid
  • Morphine poisoning — drug of choice0.4–0.8 mg IV every 2–3 min, max 10 mg
  • Neonatal asphyxiaopioids given in labour — 10 µg/kg into the umbilical cord
  • Other opioid overdoseincluding agonist-antagonists, except buprenorphine
Othersmaller doses
  • Postoperative respiratory depression0.1–0.2 mg IV, usually keeping the analgesia
  • Adjunct to intraspinal opioids
  • Diagnosis of opioid dependenceprecipitates withdrawal
  • Partly reverses alcohol intoxication
  • Tried in shock, stroke, spinal injuryvalue uncertain
For your clinicIf an opioid like fentanyl is part of IV sedation, keep naloxone ready next to flumazenil. Naloxone reverses the opioid; flumazenil reverses the benzodiazepine. Watch the patient afterwards: naloxone wears off in 1–3 h.

Other antagonists

Enter the brainNaloxone·Naltrexone·Nalmefeneblock central and peripheral μ actions
Stay outside the brainMethylnaltrexone·Alvimopanblock gut μ actions only, so central analgesia is kept

Naltrexone

Oral, lasts 1–2 days

More potent than naloxone. Opioid blockade therapy for post-addicts, 50 mg/day: a usual opioid shot gives no high and craving fades.

Also cuts alcohol craving — approved to prevent relapse of heavy drinking. Nausea, headache; hepatotoxic at high doses.

Nalmefene

Longest acting

No hepatotoxicity, better oral bioavailability, longer acting than naltrexone.

Methylnaltrexone

Peripheral only

Doesn't cross the blood-brain barrier. Reverses constipation in cancer patients on chronic opioids and in methadone maintenance.

Alvimopan

Peripheral only

Poorly absorbed from the gut and doesn't enter the brain. For postoperative ileus after bowel resection.
